## 0. File-function and controls

### 0.1 File-function

`File_57` argues that selected biblical lists can preserve two simultaneous functions:

1. a surface historical, genealogical, military, or ritual register;
2. a secondary numerical ledger that reproduces the mathematical grammar governing biblical genealogy and chronology.

The file does not claim that all biblical lists are ciphers. It isolates lists that supply multiple internal controls: overlapping subtotals and totals, ambiguous inclusion states, named but unnumbered leaders, exact calendrical conversions, repeated results in independent lists, and placement at major narrative transitions.

The principal case is 1 Chronicles 12:18–40. Numbers 28–29 supplies the earlier compact sacrificial-list precedent, while File_58 controls its full corpus treatment. First Chronicles 7:1–5 supplies an independent Issachar / `364` witness. First Chronicles 24 and 27 then extend the Davidic list grammar into priestly and annual military ordering.

The conceptual precedent is Genesis itself. Genesis 5 and 11 are lists of named persons whose stated begetting ages and lifespans generate chronology. `File_57` asks whether later bounded lists reuse that same list-to-time grammar.

Argument-control rule:
Textual data and arithmetic facts carry the base layer. Structural inference may support the argument when the active ledger, list boundary, operator, and node-class are explicit. Probable authorial design, historical-critical hypotheses, typological readings, and providential synchronizations explain higher-order meaning but do not replace arithmetic or source control.

## 1. The selected-list principle

### 1.1 Some lists, not all

A biblical list becomes a serious list-chronology candidate only when several controls converge. `File_57` uses the following selection criteria:

1. The list is textually bounded.
2. The text supplies more numerical information than ordinary identification strictly requires.
3. Inclusion or overlap is left unresolved or can be read in more than one legitimate ledger state.
4. The same number or operator recurs elsewhere in the list.
5. Exact chronological or calendrical constants emerge without rounding.
6. The list’s literary setting concerns time, priesthood, kingship, sanctuary, exile, restoration, or covenant order.
7. An independent biblical or ancient witness reproduces the same grammar.
8. Awkward numbers are accounted for rather than ignored.

No single clean factorization is sufficient. A serious argument requires a network of mutually constraining results.

### 1.2 Three primary ledgers

| Ledger | Governing question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Personnel ledger | How many people or troops are represented without duplication? | Jehoiada’s `3700` may be contained within Levi’s `4600`. |
| Printed-number ledger | What total appears if every displayed numeral is added? | Levi `4600` + Jehoiada `3700` + Zadok’s `22`. |
| Reconciliation ledger | What total appears when a textually signaled key transforms the base? | `220800 × 25/23 = 240000`. |

A fourth state, the key-withheld ledger, is used where the key is visible in the text but must remain outside the subtotal it transforms.

### 1.2A Source-order, category-partition, and declared category-order states

A bounded list may carry more than one legitimate sequence layer:

| Layer | Governing question | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Source order | In what order are the rows printed? | Preserve as textual datum. |

## 4A. Cycle Frequency Analysis

### 4A.1 Methodology
The cycle patterns documented in §§4.2–4.5 were tested against two null models to determine whether the observed density of clean multiples exceeds chance expectation. The test universe consists of all 378 contiguous spans formed by 28 endpoints (Adam through Conquest) in the Terah = 70 scaffold (grand total 2700 years, 27 intervals).

Critical constraint: Because all 27 intervals are multiples of 5, every contiguous span is also a multiple of 5. This means any cycle not divisible by 5 trivially produces zero hits. The corrected analytical expectation for a cycle C (where 5 | C) is 378 × 5/C, not the naive 378/C.

Two null models were employed:

Permutation (order test): Shuffles the 27 actual interval values, preserving the multiset but randomizing arrangement. Tests whether the specific ordering of intervals produces unusual cycle alignment.
Random mod-5 (value test): Generates random compositions of 27 positive multiples of 5 summing to 2700. Tests whether the specific values chosen (not just their order) produce unusual structure.
Both models preserve the mod-5 constraint. Results are reported from 100000 iterations each.

Because both null models preserve the mod-5 constraint, any cycle that exceeds expectation does so relative to a baseline that already accounts for the rounding operation. The scan cannot “discover” mod-5 structure as a tautological artifact of the rounding; it can only detect which specific multiples of 5 occur more frequently than other multiples of 5.

Interpretive note (important caveat): The tables below report raw Z-scores and p-values from permutation and mod-5 null tests. With 81 cycles scanned, individual p-values cannot be treated as conclusive after multiple-comparison correction. The value of these results lies in identifying which cycles occur more frequently than expected, directing attention to patterns worth investigating. Future work with formal multiple-comparison procedures (e.g., false discovery rate control) may sharpen these findings. The frequently occurring cycles should be understood as “appearing more often than expected under the null” rather than “proven non-random.”

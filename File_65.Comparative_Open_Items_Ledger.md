## 1. Evidence-accounting method

The file's unifying discipline is not that every observation proves one thesis. It is that every observation is priced before it is used.

### 1.1 Independence

One fact with several faces is not several witnesses. A translated rail, midpoint decomposition, factorisation, or uniform-width preservation may be illuminating without adding independent evidence.

### 1.2 Entailment

A result is entailed when it follows mechanically from an already admitted state and operator. Entailed displays may explain structure, but they must not be promoted into new source witnesses.

Examples in this file include:

- the preserved seven-year widths of the `1290` and `1335` famine translations;
- the multiple `45` displays generated by `1335 − 1290`;
- the `16 × 70` factorisation after the `130 + 430 + 430 + 130` frame is admitted;
- the `5 × 70`, `8 × 70`, and `130 + 350` consequences of the independently generated `886n BC` node.

### 1.3 Selection cost

A span computed only after selecting among adjacent anchors, calendar phases, or variant states carries a selection cost. The cost must be stated at the point of use; it cannot be recovered from a finished elegant display.

The principal local state hierarchy reduces rather than hides selection:

1. Athaliah's seventh-year boundary leads the schematic Judah ledger.
2. Athaliah's six completed years remain as an audit, not a coequal target choice.
3. Nisan is the default calendar.
4. The half-year is opened only where the terminal quarter-reign phase itself carries proof burden.
5. `561 BC` is preferred from historical chronology; the symmetry then tests that choice rather than generating it ex nihilo.

### 1.4 Source-state hierarchy

The file distinguishes:

1. direct textual duration, date-string, or lexical data;
2. parent-controlled chronology states;
3. locally derived comparison coordinates;
4. exact-rational completions;
5. structural inferences;
6. plausible scribal-design hypotheses;
7. typological or providential readings;
8. exploratory or dependency-blocked notes.

A later level may interpret an earlier one, but it must not replace it.

### 1.5 Translation continuity

A variant translation may preserve the same structural grammar at a different event-state. That continuity is stronger than a free numerical match but weaker than identity of historical event. The shared `1876 BC` label is the principal example.

### 1.6 Negative results

A failed route is retained when it meaningfully constrains the hypothesis.

- The standard Jacob final-life rail does not produce the two flanking `1290` columns without localized `−33`; that negative result limits the expanded lattice but does not erase the regular-MT central `1876 → 586 = 1290`.
- No MT Creation-node Jubilee boundary lands exactly on the northern fall field.

- The selected `726/586/446` symmetry is not evidence that all three years are equally fixed historical anchors.
- The BHS apparatus does not independently prove the twelfth-year reading of Ezek 33:21; it records a genuine competing tradition.
